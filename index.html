The hook · Honors thesis

Sculpture in Motion: No Gears, No Linkages

A kinetic sculpture that moves using only a material that remembers its shape. I designed and built it end to end — concept, material choice, laser-cut parts, soldered heater circuits and a screwed-together final assembly.

01Hero video — the sculpture moving

The concept

Material as mechanism.A 3D-printed shape-memory polymer is set into a temporary shape, then heated to snap back — the material does the job gears and linkages usually do.
Heat on command.Nichrome wire embedded during printing turns every panel into its own electrically driven actuator.
The story.The landscape joins Nepal’s terrain and New York City’s, linked by the Koshi and Hudson rivers; the blue panels over the river move when heated.

The context

RoleSole designer, researcher & builder
TeamIndividual honors thesis
Timeline30 weeks
ToolsFusion 360 · Bambu Studio · Laser cutter · Soldering iron · Arduino

Problem & constraints

  • No conventional mechanisms: every bit of motion had to come from the material itself.
  • Even heating: uneven temperature makes a panel bend only partway, or unpredictably.
  • Built to repeat: many identical moving panels, so every step had to give the same result each time.

Process map — how I built it

  1. 01ConceptTerrains, river link, panel layout
  2. 02MaterialPLA + TPU shape-memory panels
  3. 03CADFusion 360 models + wire fixture
  4. 04SimulateWire count & spacing vs. ΔT
  5. 05Print + embedNichrome laid in mid-print
  6. 06Laser-cutBase and panels to shape
  7. 07Solder + screwWire circuits; screwed joints
  8. 08Control & testArduino + temperature sensor

Photos & CAD

Highlights

  • Simulation before fabrication: the best wire layout was found before printing, so fewer test prints were wasted.
  • Fixture-driven repeatability: the fixture places the wire the same way in every panel.
  • Modular, serviceable build: screwed — not glued — joints let any panel be swapped without a rebuild.
  • Production flow: print → embed wire → laser-cut → solder → assemble, repeated for each panel.
The results

10KConstrained in max ΔT at optimal spacing

A working kinetic sculpture that moves without gears or linkages, built from repeatable shape-memory panels with embedded heaters.

A carbon-fiber 3D-printed brace that mechanically locks out hyperextension and auto-adjusts compression from pressure-sensor feedback. [Impact metric: e.g. “holds X° stop, adjusts compression in Y s”]

Problem & constraints

  • Sports regulations: the design must stay legal for competitive play.

High-Performance Electric Dragster

Modeled an optimal 4.7 s race time, then traded it against braking, cost and manufacturability to ship a 2:1 AWD build with a servo brake triggered at ~385 in. [Add actual race result: X.X s]

03Hero image — dragster CAD render or on track

The context

RoleChassis & wheels lead · manufacturing planner
Team4 engineers
Timeline[One semester]
ToolsCNC mill · lathe · waterjet · 3D printing · laser cutter

Problem & constraints

  • Competing goals: acceleration vs. braking vs. controllability vs. manufacturability.
  • Cost penalties: each extra battery added cost that offset its speed gain.
  • Stopping distance: braking had to engage reliably at a fixed track position.

Process map — manufacturing routing

  1. CNCAluminum brake stopTight-tolerance fit
  2. LATHEAluminum axlesRoundness + custom geometry
  3. JETWaterjet chassisPrecision profile
  4. TPUPrinted wheelsTraction
  5. PLAHubs, servo mount, brake partsFast iteration
  6. LASERPlywood battery mountLight, quick to cut

Photos & CAD

Highlights

  • All-wheel drive: the motor gear drives the front axle and a belt and pulleys carry power to the rear, so all four wheels push.
  • 2:1 fixed gearing: close to the 2.1 modeled optimum, balancing launch acceleration against top speed.
  • Programmed braking: an Arduino-timed servo drives a linear stop into the axle stop at ~385 in.
  • One battery, by design: a second battery gained ~2 s but lost it to the penalty and cost.
  • Grip and precision: treaded TPU wheels, a CNC-milled brake stop, lathe-turned axles and a waterjet-cut chassis.

Analysis-driven decisions on gear ratio, battery count and drivetrain, plus a six-process manufacturing plan, delivered a working AWD dragster on schedule.
